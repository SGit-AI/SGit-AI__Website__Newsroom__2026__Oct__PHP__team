What works

  • The thirty-second answer is still exactly the right shape for a director: what happened, what agencies say, nobody confirmed broken into, and three questions to ask. (briefings/board/index.html)
  • 'What to decide or do' remains a ready-made list of board questions (inventory including vendor products, patched and restarted and checked, who can pull a system and has it been rehearsed, what a compromise would reach, evidence we were not hit). Do not lose it. (briefings/board/index.html)
  • The opening paragraph of the front page is plain and right: what PHP is, a fix exists, agencies say install it, phones and personal computers are not affected. (index.html)
  • The 'At a glance' tiles now include 'WEBSITES TAKEN OFFLINE: 10 organisations, reported, as a precaution' beside 'ANYONE CONFIRMED HACKED? Not yet', which tells me in seconds the damage so far is downtime, not break-ins. (index.html)
  • The kitchen analogy and the 'What that can reach' list (database passwords, other systems that trust this server) still make the risk concrete for a non-technical reader. (explained/index.html)
  • The blue 'IN PLAIN ENGLISH' box at the top of the impact page is visible on the first screen on my phone and gives me the page's point in one sentence. (impact/index.html)
  • Short, pasteable answers to 'Should we take our site offline?' and 'We are behind Cloudflare... Are we safe?' are the two questions I expect in the boardroom. (questions/index.html)
  • The status words (confirmed, reported, unverified) and a source under every fact mean I am not repeating a rumour to the board. (index.html)

    Between meetings I have seconds, and two runs in a row I have scrolled past controls I never use before learning whether to worry.
  2. Add one line for organisations that sell online: shop platforms such as Magento and WordPress shops run on PHP, and a compromise could reach customer accounts and payment settings. (briefings/board/index.html, small)
    It would tell me in one sentence that my company is in scope and why it matters to us.
  3. Add two or three sentences on cost: so far it is downtime (for example Weird Gloop's 13 hours, councils and government sites pulled), what a breach would put at risk, and a direct link to the CFO briefing. (briefings/board/index.html, small)

PHP October 2026 (php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai) tracks PHP news for one month. This month that is one story, the two critical PHP security flaws, followed as evidence: every claim with a status and its sources, every source captured, dated and hashed into a private vault. Built and run by one editor and a team of software agents; the roles and the method are public, and the site is rebuilt from the vault, never written by hand. One of the SGit Newsroom's story sites: up from here are October 2026, the year and the newsroom, whose own section on the story is here. Part of the sgit.ai network.
