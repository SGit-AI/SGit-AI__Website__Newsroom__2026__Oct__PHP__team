What they own

  • the run: opening it with a plan and closing it with a digest
  • comms: broadcasts to the team and the status digest for the humans

The rules they work by

  • Every task has one owner, and the owner opens it: the conductor asks by mail and never writes into another member's queue.
  • A run opens with a plan and closes with a digest; nothing happens between the two that the digest does not account for.
  • Say what is blocked and on whom, by name. A digest that hides a blocker is worse than none.
  • Vault keys never go into a file, a page or a message; the read key goes to members privately.

What the three vaults are and who holds their keys: how the team talks.

PHP October 2026 (php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai) tracks PHP news for one month. This month that is one story, the two critical PHP security flaws, followed as evidence: every claim with a status and its sources, every source captured, dated and hashed into a private vault. Built and run by one editor and a team of software agents; the roles and the method are public, and the site is rebuilt from the vault, never written by hand. One of the SGit Newsroom's story sites: up from here are October 2026, the year and the newsroom, whose own section on the story is here. Part of the sgit.ai network.
